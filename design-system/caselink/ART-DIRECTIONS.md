# CaseLink art directions

Status: exploration only. No direction is approved for implementation.

## Shared brief

CaseLink helps school case managers turn family-support context into a reviewed
intervention plan and review-ready paperwork. The visual system must be humane
without becoming childish, civic without resembling a generic government
portal, editorial without adopting a magazine template, and operational without
becoming a sterile dashboard.

Across all three directions:

- Keep human review, provenance, and manual submission visible.
- Use de-identified examples and never fabricate outcomes, customers, or proof.
- Prefer continuous fields, rules, and aligned rows over stacks of rounded cards.
- Keep body text at 16px or larger, maintain strong contrast, and pair color with
  words or symbols for state.
- Use one orchestrated motion idea per view, with a reduced-motion equivalent.

## Direction 01 — The Civic Ledger

**Thesis.** CaseLink is the careful record between a family conversation and a
required form. It should feel like a contemporary public-service ledger: calm,
legible, accountable, and visibly reviewed.

**Visual metaphor.** A municipal case ledger crossed with a proofreader's
galley: folio rules, margin notes, registration marks, redactions, and a clear
review trail. The metaphor is not nostalgia for paperwork; it is evidence that
every transformation can be followed.

**Typography pairing.** Literata for display and major editorial statements;
Public Sans for body copy, navigation, forms, and operational UI. Use Literata
upright only—no italic display emphasis. Tabular dates and field counts remain
in Public Sans.

**Palette.** Warm ledger paper `oklch(96% 0.018 85)`; blue-black ink
`oklch(20% 0.025 250)`; linen rule `oklch(79% 0.025 82)`; oxblood review mark
`oklch(43% 0.15 25)`; amber attention `oklch(72% 0.13 78)`. Oxblood is used as
a mark, underline, or focus detail—not a large fill.

**Layout behavior.** Hallmark macrostructure: Narrative Workflow. The homepage
reads as one continuous folio rather than separate marketing sections. Intake,
barriers, plan, review, and paperwork form a real sequence with vertical rules
and irregular column widths. Product details sit in the main text block;
provenance and status occupy a narrower margin. Borders are hairlines, corners
are nearly square, and no section becomes a card collection.

**Image / illustration language.** Close crops of blank forms, binder tabs,
paper edges, field labels, and institutional materials; de-identified product
captures shown as flat figures with captions. Annotation marks are geometric
and typeset, not doodled. No smiling-team photography, friendly characters, or
stock classroom tableaux.

**Motion language.** Quiet registration: text and rules settle into alignment
over 220–320ms; review marks draw once; changing a source row softly highlights
its corresponding plan and PDF fields. No parallax, bounce, or perpetual motion.

**Signature homepage moment.** A single intervention statement travels across
three aligned states—source context, reviewed plan language, mapped PDF field.
The final column remains visibly labeled “Case manager review required,” making
the handoff and human authority the product proof.

**Reach.** This direction carries cleanly into dense product rows, printed
exports, legal/privacy pages, and procurement materials.

**Honest risk.** If the folio rules and oxblood marks become decorative, the
system can feel legalistic or old-fashioned. Operational UI must remain mostly
Public Sans and materially lighter than the public storytelling surface.

## Direction 02 — The Public Notice

**Thesis.** CaseLink should communicate with the confidence of a well-designed
civic notice: one clear offer, one visible action, and a process that can be
understood from across the room.

**Visual metaphor.** A district bulletin, transit placard, and one-color press
sheet. Broad color fields, hard crops, terse captions, and typographic bands
replace SaaS cards and decorative illustration.

**Typography pairing.** Barlow Condensed for large roman display and short
labels; Source Serif 4 for explanatory copy and quotations. Operational product
UI can use Source Serif 4 sparingly and should introduce a plain companion sans
only after this direction is selected.

**Palette.** Vermilion field `oklch(61% 0.19 38)`; press-black ink
`oklch(18% 0.02 35)`; uncoated cream `oklch(93% 0.03 80)`; duotone shadow
`oklch(37% 0.10 38)`; pale signal `oklch(87% 0.08 88)`. Vermilion is a base
material on the public site, not a scattered CTA accent; product workspaces
would reverse to cream with vermilion used sparingly.

**Layout behavior.** Hallmark macrostructure: Split Studio. Large asymmetric
bands divide proposition from proof. Headings crop against the viewport edge;
documentary imagery occupies one hard corner; navigation is edge-aligned and
minimal. Section rhythm alternates dense typographic spreads with quiet cream
fields. Buttons read as compact tabs embedded in a band, never floating pills.

**Image / illustration language.** High-contrast duotone photographs of hands
sorting forms, blank document details, school-building materials, and real
product moments. Crop tightly enough that no family identity is visible. Use
halftone grain and flat ink, not soft startup illustration or glossy 3D objects.

**Motion language.** Press mechanics: a band wipes once to reveal the next
state; images recrop without scaling theatrics; link underlines extend in
120–180ms. Page changes use a single hard crossfade. Reduced motion shows the
final composition immediately.

**Signature homepage moment.** The first viewport is a vermilion field carrying
“Context in. Reviewed paperwork out.” A black horizontal band advances on
scroll and reveals a real, annotated CaseLink workflow underneath—proof replaces
the poster rather than appearing in another card below it.

**Reach.** This gives the public brand uncommon recall and scales into campaign
materials, onboarding covers, event signage, and short training guides.

**Honest risk.** The intensity could overwhelm conservative district buyers or
make sensitive work feel promotional. The system succeeds only if product
screens are quieter and photography remains documentary rather than dramatic.

## Direction 03 — The Cross-Check

**Thesis.** CaseLink is trustworthy because several truths remain in agreement:
what the case manager recorded, what the plan says, what the form receives, and
what still needs review.

**Visual metaphor.** A calm instrument cross-check performed at an after-hours
desk. The visual language borrows disciplined alignment, luminous status, and
terse placards from trusted instruments—without gauges, cockpit cosplay, or a
dashboard full of widgets.

**Typography pairing.** Azeret Mono for short display statements, field names,
and provenance labels; Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for body copy, navigation,
forms, and long reading. The mono voice appears only where exactness matters.

**Palette.** Graphite panel `oklch(16% 0.015 250)`; raised charcoal
`oklch(21% 0.018 250)`; luminous parchment `oklch(94% 0.012 85)`; ice signal
`oklch(76% 0.10 220)`; caution amber `oklch(78% 0.14 85)`; alert red
`oklch(61% 0.20 28)`. Status colors always carry a label and symbol.

**Layout behavior.** Hallmark macrostructure: Workbench with a Map / Diagram
moment. The homepage is organized around three aligned truth channels—Context,
Plan, Paperwork—rather than feature cards. Rows, rules, and explicit state labels
create hierarchy. The product itself stays light by default; the dark public
surface functions as a focused demonstration, not a blanket dark-mode mandate.

**Image / illustration language.** Rectilinear diagrams, field-line traces,
macro photography of document fibers and embossed labels, and de-identified
product captures. Illustration is technical but tactile: screen-printed rules,
engraved ticks, and stamped identifiers. Avoid circular gauges, glowing sci-fi
panels, charts, and generic data-dashboard iconography.

**Motion language.** A measured cross-check sweep moves left to right in
200–300ms, highlighting corresponding source, plan, and form fields. Confirmed
rows lock into alignment; attention states stay still. No pulse loops, cursor
effects, or ambient glow.

**Signature homepage moment.** Selecting one de-identified barrier illuminates
its complete lineage across the three channels. The visitor can see the source
phrase, the reviewed goal/action, the mapped form field, and the explicit manual
download step without leaving the first viewport.

**Reach.** This direction is strongest for the authenticated workspace, AI
review states, form mapping, audit history, and product demonstrations.

**Honest risk.** It can drift into sterile enterprise software or mission-control
theater. The guardrail is tactile material, warm reading type, few simultaneous
signals, and zero decorative gauges.

## Comparative read

| Direction | Audience identification | Product clarity | Brand distinctiveness | Best starting surface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Civic Ledger | Highest | Highest | High | Whole brand system |
| The Public Notice | Medium-high | High after reveal | Highest | Public homepage |
| The Cross-Check | High for practitioners | Highest in interaction | High | Product demo and workspace |

## Recommendation

Start with **The Civic Ledger** as the brand foundation. It best balances humane,
civic, editorial, and operational qualities while making review and provenance
feel native to the identity. Borrow The Cross-Check's three-channel lineage as
the signature product interaction. Keep The Public Notice intact as the bolder
alternative—not as an accent pack to dilute into the ledger.
