## Behavior

New plans are drafted in one compact response, grouped by goals and exact proposed dates. Existing unfinished staged drafts retain their recovery path. Generation, refinement and the assistant share saved family goals, household context, dated meetings and notes; restrictions are not truncated to fit. Source descriptions help distinguish an appropriate service from a superficial category match. Missing facts stay unknown, and directory edits invalidate responses that used stale evidence, even without a contact card.

The assistant offers email-draft, call-script and meeting-update prompts. These prepare text for staff review; they do not contact anyone, change the plan, or mark work done. Full-plan refinement is explicitly limited to supported changes on existing actions. Manual editing remains available for structural changes.

A failed AI draft now offers **Continue manually**. The authorized transaction checks the displayed plan revision, preserves saved work, ends the AI job, cancels its lease and records a distinct manual-handoff event. A late response cannot overwrite it. Empty drafts still need an action, and date confirmation, human review and current resource checks still gate PDF export.

The Luna rerun was substantially cache-warmed; costs are observed totals, not equal-cache forecasts. Timings exclude hosting, database and browser work. Sol's p75 of 15.85 seconds slightly exceeds the PRD's 15-second service target; its p95 is below the 30-second target. Because this response is saved atomically, the first useful actions appear with the complete draft, so the 5/10-second first-action targets are also not met in this sample. The decision favors demonstrated output quality over the older model's speed, and does not claim every latency target passed. For the five substantive narrow edits, Sol took p75 8.99 seconds versus Luna 3.71 seconds; estimated cost was $0.04060 versus $0.00184. Both had zero cache reads and the same cache-write input count. This small service-only sample indicates about 0.8 cents more per edit, not a production forecast.
